4.1 Reproduction

4.1.1 Reproduction experiments

Our earthworm breeding system is described Section 2.1.1.1.

The soil used for all experiments is the same as the culture to be as close as possible to the conditions of the natural population.

For all experiments, Imidacloprid PESTANAL®, analytical standard (ref 37894-100MG) and Epoxiconazole regarder flacon au N1 (ref ) from Sigma-Aldrich were used. Due to the very low water solubility of epoxiconazole and the relatively high tested concentrations, it was not possible to spike the soil with a water solution containing both epoxiconazole and imidacloprid. The alternative use of a co-solvent was avoided, as they are often toxic to earthworms, and the required quantity would have been excessive. One day prior to the start of the tests, the soil contamination was carried out in two stages: first, a dry mixture of epoxiconazole and soil at 20% of its WHC was prepared, followed by spiking the soil with a water solution of imidacloprid.

Reproduction tests were conducted following an adaptation of the OECD Technical Guideline 222 (OECD, 2016) in a two-stage experiment. Each pesticide was first tested individually (Experiment A) before assessing the mixture (Experiment B).

To study the effects of individual pesticides, tested concentrations were selected based on the ecotoxicological data from Lewis et al. (2006). For Eisenia fetida, the reported acute 14-day LC50 values were 10.7 mg/kg for imidacloprid and >500 mg/kg for epoxiconazole, while the chronic reproduction NOEC values were ≥3.24 mg/kg and ≥0.178 mg/kg, respectively. An initial set of eight concentrations was selected for each pesticide, arranged in a geometric sequence to cover the full response range. For imidacloprid, an additional set of three concentrations, including one already tested, was added to refine the dose-response curve. Each concentration was tested in five replicates.

For each replicate, two earthworms were placed in a cosm consisting of a plastic vessel (11 cm × 7.7 cm × 4.5 cm) containing the equivalent of 200 g of dry soil. The exposure lasted 28 days at 18°C. Earthworms were weighed at the start and end of the experiment and were fed ad libitum with horse dung (3 g per individual every 14 days).

Soil moisture was checked and adjusted weekly. At the end of the experiments, earthworms were starved on wet filter paper in Petri dishes to empty their gut contents before being frozen at -80°C. Cocoons produced during the experiments were retrieved by wet-sieving the soil through a 1-mm mesh (Bart, 2018) and incubated at 18°C in Petri dishes on wet filter paper (Holmstrup et al., 1991). Cocoon hatching was monitored each day of the working week for 96 days for imidacloprid and 55 for epoxiconazole.

A more detailed description of the experiment procedure is available in protocols\2_EC50_tests and the temporality of all experiments is described in a GANTT chart Section 1.2.2.

4.1.2 Experimental results

Variables names :

  • Date : Date of the measurement
  • ID_cosm : Identification number of the cosm
  • t : Number of days since the start of the exposition (d)
  • w_tot : Fresh weight of the earthworms in one cosm (mg)
  • Nb_ind : Number of alive individuals in a cosm (#)
  • Dose : concentration in the soil (mg/kg)
  • Status :
    • “J” : Juvenile
    • “A” : Adulte
    • “D” : Dead

4.1.2.2 Dose-response model

Dose-response curves are modeled with the Hill model (Equation 4.1, Figure 4.2).

\[ Y=Y_{min}+\frac{Y_{max}-Y_{min}}{1+\text{exp}(Slope\times [\text{Ln}(Dose)-\text{Ln}(EC_{50})])} \tag{4.1}\]

With :

  • \(Y\) : Response for a given dose,
  • \(Y_{min}\) : Minimal response,
  • \(Y_{max}\) : Maximal response,
  • \(EC_{50}\) : Dose for which the response equals \((Y_{max}-Y_{min})/2\),
  • \(Slope\) : Slope when the dose equals the EC\(_{50}\).

4.1.2.3 Effect on cocoon production

For reproduction, the minimal response is obviously 0. Therefore, we set \(Y_{min}\) to 0 in our model.

4.2 Juvenile growth

4.2.1 Growth experiment

Juveniles used in these tests were collected in the culture with similar body weights. Juvenile growth tests followed the same pattern as reproduction tests. The study of the effects of individual pesticides was performed using the same doses as in the reproduction tests.

For each replicate, one earthworm was placed in a cosm consisting of a plastic vessel (11 cm × 7.7 cm × 4.5 cm) containing the equivalent of 200 g of dry soil. The exposure lasted 28 days at 18°C. Earthworms were weighed at the start, after 14 days and at the end of the experiment (28 days) and were fed ad libitum with horse dung (3 g per individual every 14 days). Soil moisture was checked and adjusted weekly. At the end of the experiments, earthworms were starved on wet filter paper in Petri dishes to empty their gut contents before being frozen at -80°C.

4.2.2 Experimental results

Variables names :

  • Date : Date of the measurement
  • ID : Identification number of the earthworm
  • t : Number of days since the start of the exposition
  • w : Fresh weight of the earthworm (mg)
  • Dose : concentration in the soil (mg/kg)
  • status :
    • “J” : Juvenile
    • “A” : Adulte
    • “D” : Dead

4.2.3 Effect on juvenile growth

4.2.3.1 Growth model - Growth rates calculation

The growth model is the following :

\[ \frac{dL}{dt}=a \Rightarrow L(t)=L_0 + a\times t \]

With :

  • L : The structural volumetric length of the earthworm (\(= \sqrt[3]{w}\)) (mg\(^{1/3}\))
  • a : Growth rate (mg\(^{1/3}\)/d)

For each earthworm, the growth rate is estimated using nls from the “stats” package.

Code 
f_growth_rate <- function(data){
  a_init <- 0.1
  a_i <- c()
  
  # Calculation of the growth rate for each individual
  for(i in subset(data, t==0)$ID){
    data_i <- data.frame(
      L = subset(data, ID==i)$L,
      t = subset(data, ID==i)$t
      )
    L0_i <- subset(data, ID==i & t==0)$L

      a.Est <- (subset(data_i, !t==0)$L-subset(data_i, t==0)$L)/
        subset(data_i, !t==0)$t
    }else{ # If 3 data points (or more)
      EPX.nlsHill=nls(
        L ~ L0_i+t*a,
        start = list(a=a_init), 
        trace=T, 
        data=data_i
        ) 
      a.Est <- EPX.nlsHill$m$getPars()[1]
    }
    a_i <- rbind(a_i, a.Est)
  }

  return(a_i)
}

# Effects of single exposures on earthworms

## Reproduction

### Reproduction experiments

Our earthworm breeding system is described @sec-earthwormsbreeding.

The soil used for all experiments is the same as the culture to be as close as possible to the conditions of the natural population.

For all experiments, Imidacloprid PESTANAL®, analytical standard (ref 37894-100MG) and Epoxiconazole regarder flacon au N1 (ref ) from Sigma-Aldrich were used. Due to the very low water solubility of epoxiconazole and the relatively high tested concentrations, it was not possible to spike the soil with a water solution containing both epoxiconazole and imidacloprid. The alternative use of a co-solvent was avoided, as they are often toxic to earthworms, and the required quantity would have been excessive. One day prior to the start of the tests, the soil contamination was carried out in two stages: first, a dry mixture of epoxiconazole and soil at 20% of its WHC was prepared, followed by spiking the soil with a water solution of imidacloprid.

Reproduction tests were conducted following an adaptation of the OECD Technical Guideline 222 [@oecd_2016] in a two-stage experiment. Each pesticide was first tested individually (Experiment A) before assessing the mixture (Experiment B).

To study the effects of individual pesticides, tested concentrations were selected based on the ecotoxicological data from @lewis_2006. For Eisenia fetida, the reported acute 14-day LC50 values were 10.7 mg/kg for imidacloprid and \>500 mg/kg for epoxiconazole, while the chronic reproduction NOEC values were ≥3.24 mg/kg and ≥0.178 mg/kg, respectively. An initial set of eight concentrations was selected for each pesticide, arranged in a geometric sequence to cover the full response range. For imidacloprid, an additional set of three concentrations, including one already tested, was added to refine the dose-response curve. Each concentration was tested in five replicates.

For each replicate, two earthworms were placed in a cosm consisting of a plastic vessel (11 cm × 7.7 cm × 4.5 cm) containing the equivalent of 200 g of dry soil. The exposure lasted 28 days at 18°C. Earthworms were weighed at the start and end of the experiment and were fed ad libitum with horse dung (3 g per individual every 14 days).

Soil moisture was checked and adjusted weekly. At the end of the experiments, earthworms were starved on wet filter paper in Petri dishes to empty their gut contents before being frozen at -80°C. Cocoons produced during the experiments were retrieved by wet-sieving the soil through a 1-mm mesh [@bart_2018] and incubated at 18°C in Petri dishes on wet filter paper [@holmstrup_1991]. Cocoon hatching was monitored each day of the working week for 96 days for imidacloprid and 55 for epoxiconazole.

A more detailed description of the experiment procedure is available in `protocols\2_EC50_tests` and the temporality of all experiments is described in a GANTT chart @sec-GANTT.

#### Dose-response model

Dose-response curves are modeled with the Hill model (@eq-Hill, @fig-drcscheme).

$$
Y=Y_{min}+\frac{Y_{max}-Y_{min}}{1+\text{exp}(Slope\times [\text{Ln}(Dose)-\text{Ln}(EC_{50})])}
$$ {#eq-Hill}

With :

-   $Y$ : Response for a given dose,
-   $Y_{min}$ : Minimal response,
-   $Y_{max}$ : Maximal response,
-   $EC_{50}$ : Dose for which the response equals $(Y_{max}-Y_{min})/2$,
-   $Slope$ : Slope when the dose equals the EC$_{50}$.

## Juvenile growth

### Growth experiment

Juveniles used in these tests were collected in the culture with similar body weights. Juvenile growth tests followed the same pattern as reproduction tests. The study of the effects of individual pesticides was performed using the same doses as in the reproduction tests.

For each replicate, one earthworm was placed in a cosm consisting of a plastic vessel (11 cm × 7.7 cm × 4.5 cm) containing the equivalent of 200 g of dry soil. The exposure lasted 28 days at 18°C. Earthworms were weighed at the start, after 14 days and at the end of the experiment (28 days) and were fed ad libitum with horse dung (3 g per individual every 14 days). Soil moisture was checked and adjusted weekly. At the end of the experiments, earthworms were starved on wet filter paper in Petri dishes to empty their gut contents before being frozen at -80°C.

### Experimental results

Variables names :

-   `Date` : Date of the measurement
-   `ID` : Identification number of the earthworm
-   `t` : Number of days since the start of the exposition
-   `w` : Fresh weight of the earthworm (mg)
-   `Dose` : concentration in the soil (mg/kg)
-   `status` :
    -   "J" : Juvenile
    -   "A" : Adulte
    -   "D" : Dead

### Effect on juvenile growth

#### Growth model - Growth rates calculation

The growth model is the following :

$$
\frac{dL}{dt}=a \Rightarrow L(t)=L_0 + a\times t
$$

With :

-   L : The structural volumetric length of the earthworm ($= \sqrt[3]{w}$) (mg$^{1/3}$)
-   a : Growth rate (mg$^{1/3}$/d)

For each earthworm, the growth rate is estimated using `nls` from the "stats" package.

```{r growthratesfuncg}
f_growth_rate <- function(data){
  a_init <- 0.1
  a_i <- c()
  
  # Calculation of the growth rate for each individual
  for(i in subset(data, t==0)$ID){
    data_i <- data.frame(
      L = subset(data, ID==i)$L,
      t = subset(data, ID==i)$t
      )
    L0_i <- subset(data, ID==i & t==0)$L
    
    if(length(na.omit(data_i$L))==2){ # If only 2 data points
      a.Est <- (subset(data_i, !t==0)$L-subset(data_i, t==0)$L)/
        subset(data_i, !t==0)$t
    }else{ # If 3 data points (or more)
      EPX.nlsHill=nls(
        L ~ L0_i+t*a,
        start = list(a=a_init), 
        trace=T, 
        data=data_i
        ) 
      a.Est <- EPX.nlsHill$m$getPars()[1]
    }
    a_i <- rbind(a_i, a.Est)
  }

  return(a_i)
}
```
